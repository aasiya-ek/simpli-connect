Types

  • Equity Mutual Funds: Equity mutual funds invest primarily in equity shares of companies. These funds are suitable for investors who are willing to take higher risks in the hope of earning higher returns.
  • Debt Mutual Funds: Debt mutual funds invest primarily in fixed-income securities like bonds and debentures. These funds are suitable for investors who are looking for a stable source of income and are willing to take lower risks.
  • Balanced Mutual Funds: Balanced mutual funds invest in both equity and debt instruments. These funds are suitable for investors who are looking for a balance between capital appreciation and regular income.
  • Index Funds: Index funds track a specific stock market index, such as the Nifty 50 or the BSE Sensex. These funds are suitable for investors who want to invest in the stock market but do not have the expertise or time to analyze individual stocks.
  • Sectoral/Thematic Funds: Sectoral/Thematic funds invest in specific sectors or themes such as banking, technology, or healthcare. These funds are suitable for investors who have a specific investment theme in mind.
  • Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs): ETFs are similar to index funds, but they are traded on stock exchanges like individual stocks. ETFs can be bought and sold during market hours, and their prices fluctuate throughout the day.
